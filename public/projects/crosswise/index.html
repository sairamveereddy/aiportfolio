CrossWise: AI Curb Crossing Copilot for Food Delivery Robots

A multi-model vision-language crossing copilot engineered to prevent urban delivery robot freeze, pedestrian blind-spot collisions, and illegal crosswalk entries at complex city intersections.

Hackathon Event VAST Builders Challenge NYC (October 2026)
Team Identifier team-48
Open-Source Repo github.com/sairamveereddy/CROSSWISE
Operational Scope Decision Support on Multi-Camera Curb Footage (NYC & SF)

Scope & Evaluation Transparency

Hardware Notice: CrossWise was developed and evaluated as an AI software decision-support and perception copilot. It was tested against recorded multi-angle curb video streams across 7 fixed intersections in New York City and San Francisco. It did not involve physical rover chassis manufacturing or autonomous drive-by-wire steering actuation on public streets. Evaluation benchmarks reflect simulated policy replay on benchmarked video footage.

1. Project Overview & The Urban Last-Mile Problem

Autonomous sidewalk delivery robots (such as Serve, Starship, or Cartken rovers) face their greatest failure rates at curb-to-crosswalk transitions. Unlike open roadways or quiet suburban sidewalks, dense metropolitan intersections (e.g., Broadway & 42nd St in Manhattan or Market St in San Francisco) exhibit extreme occlusion:

  • Pedestrian Crowding & Ghost Stalls: Conventional proximity sensors freeze the robot indefinitely when pedestrians swarm the curb, causing delivery timeout failures.
  • Signal Misinterpretation: Standard object detection models frequently misread pedestrian signal countdowns or confuse vehicular green lights with pedestrian "WALK" heads across multi-phase signals.
  • Turning Vehicle Blind Spots: Right-turning vehicles entering crosswalks from behind the robot's field of view create severe collision hazards.

CrossWise was engineered at the VAST Builders Challenge NYC Hackathon (team-48) to act as a high-reliability crossing copilot. It continuously processes camera frames at the curb, tracks pedestrian cross-flows, evaluates signal state confidence, and outputs definitive GO, WAIT, or NO-GO decisions with automated teleoperation escalation when perception confidence drops below safety thresholds.

2. System Architecture & Perception Pipeline

CrossWise pairs high-frequency real-time object tracking with deep multimodal reasoning models running across CoreWeave GPU compute and indexed via VAST DataEngine and VastDB.

The Dual-Speed Loop:

  1. The Fast Perception Loop (0.2s): YOLO11 runs frame-by-frame on camera streams to detect pedestrian bounding boxes, velocity vectors, turning vehicles, and signal indicators.
  2. The Multimodal Reasoning Loop (CoreWeave / VastDB): NVIDIA Cosmos Reason evaluates physical plausibility and vehicle trajectories. Gemma 4 31B vision cross-verifies contradictory pedestrian signals. Llama 3.3 70B synthesizes spatial geometry to produce safety rationale.
  3. Teleoperation Escalation: If a delivery robot is immobilized at a curb for more than 30 seconds due to sensor ambiguity or crowd blockage, CrossWise triggers an automated dispatch packet to remote human operators via W&B Weave tracing.

3. Key Technologies & Infrastructure

AI Models & Compute

  • NVIDIA Cosmos Reason: Physical scene understanding and spatial trajectory prediction.
  • YOLO11: High-FPS bounding box detection for pedestrians, curbs, crosswalk markings, and cars.
  • Llama 3.3 70B: High-level policy coordination and decision arbitration.
  • Gemma 4 31B Vision: High-resolution signal head crop inspection.
  • CoreWeave GPUs: Accelerated batch inference pipeline.

Data, Storage & Observability

  • VAST DataEngine: High-throughput ingestion of multi-camera recorded footage.
  • VastDB: Fast tabular querying of frame-by-frame metadata, bounding boxes, and policy replay traces.
  • W&B Weave: Real-time LLM prompt evaluation, tracing, and decision auditing.
  • Python & OpenCV: Frame extraction, perspective transforms, and visual overlay generation.

4. Experimental Results: Frame-Level vs Segment-Level Policy Replay

During the VAST Builders Challenge hackathon, we conducted comparative replay evaluations (implemented in policy_eval.py) benchmarking CrossWise against two operational approaches:

Evaluation Metric Baseline (5s Segment LLM Replay) CrossWise (0.2s Dual-Speed Replay)
Illegal Starts Against "DON'T WALK" 3 Violations (Lagged context) 0 Violations (100% adherence)
Pedestrian Crosswalk Freeze Rate High (~42% unnecessary stops) Low (Tracks velocity vectors around robot)
Decision Latency 3,200 ms (Single slow VLM call) ~210 ms Fast Loop / Asynchronous Deep VLM
Stuck Robot Escalation Time Manual report / Unmonitored Automated 30-second teleoperation alert

*Note: Results obtained on recorded test suites comprising 7 fixed NYC and SF intersection camera clips in benchmark simulations.
