From Detection to Proficiency: The Coming Inversion of Academic AI Policy and a Framework for Assessing Student AI Utilization in Higher Education (2026–2035)

Abstract

Higher education's dominant institutional response to generative artificial intelligence has been detection: identifying and penalizing student use of large language models in assessed work. This paper argues that the detection paradigm is structurally unsustainable and will invert within a decade.

Drawing on assessment theory, distributed cognition, and empirical evidence on AI-augmented knowledge work, the paper develops the case that by the early 2030s the central assessment question will shift from "did the student use AI?" to "how well did the student use AI?"

It proposes AI Utilization Proficiency (AUP) as an assessable construct comprising six dimensions: task decomposition, prompt construction, iterative refinement, critical verification, synthesis and attribution, and tool orchestration.

The paper introduces Baseline Differential Analysis (BDA), an estimation method that infers utilization quality from submitted artifacts alone by comparing them against machine-generated minimal-effort baselines from the model the student reports using. Using scenario analysis across a 2026–2035 horizon, the paper traces three phases of transition: prohibition erosion, hybrid accommodation, and proficiency-centered assessment. It closes with implications for instructors, institutions, accreditors, and tool builders, and with the ethical constraints any utilization-scoring system must respect.

Core Framework: The 6 Dimensions of AI Utilization Proficiency (AUP)

Dimension 1

Task Decomposition

The ability to break complex analytical assignments into modular sub-tasks suitable for specific model architectures.

Dimension 2

Prompt Construction

Formulating precise system context, few-shot exemplars, structured constraints, and semantic schemas.

Dimension 3

Iterative Refinement

Multi-turn steering, critique-correction cycles, and recursive feedback loops rather than passive single-shot outputs.

Dimension 4

Critical Verification

Detecting factual hallucination, auditing mathematical derivations, and validating code against unit test harnesses.

Dimension 5

Synthesis & Attribution

Integrating machine-generated text with human cognitive insight while transparently attributing AI contribution.

Dimension 6

Tool Orchestration

Connecting foundation models to external retrieval tools, databases, Python runtimes, and citation stores.
